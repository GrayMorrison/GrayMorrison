Artist biography
Gray Morrison is a multi-instrumentalist, singer, and songwriter from Northern Ireland whose music blends raw intensity with resonant melody and fearless honesty. His sound is driven by soaring guitars steeped in the spirit of better decades, relentless rhythms, and lyrics that cut to the core of what it means to be human. Through songs that explore love and loss, memory and survival, Morrison confronts the human condition while searching for meaning in the mysteries that shape our world. Drawing on influences from rock, post-punk, folk, jazz, blues, country, and electronic music, Morrison crafts songs that are both fierce and fragile, unafraid to face the darkness yet always reaching toward the light. The name Gray Morrison is a nom de plume, drawn from the artist’s university nickname and his mother’s maiden name.

The Devil's Side

The Devil’s Side began with a small question my son asked just before he fell asleep: “Why are some people bad?” The song grew from that moment. It tells an allegorical story about a man who gains influence, slowly twists it into harm, and drifts beyond redemption while the narrator watches him slip further away. It reflects on whether our paths are shaped by fate or by the choices we make. The title is a metaphor for the slow turn toward selfishness, domination, and cruelty, instead of empathy, compassion, and kindness. The story also leaves room for the idea that some people may be pulled toward destructive behaviour no matter what, and that there can be a quiet, difficult comfort in accepting that not everything can be changed. The Devil’s Side was released on 25 September 2026 as a two‑track EP featuring both vocal and instrumental versions.

Gray Morrison (self-titled debut EP)

Recorded between March and December 2025, this collection was born from a time of upheaval, capturing the fragile balance between darkness and hope. Each track reaches toward meaning, connection, and the possibility of redemption, or the quiet acceptance of closure. Morrison is joined by Joanne Rowland on vocals (Reconciliation), longtime school friend Rosie Marc (Jayne Harkness, formerly of Vokxen) on drums and cajon (When the Party's Over, Reconciliation, The Lighthouse), David Jamison (formerly of Clay) on drums (Western Eyes) and percussion (When the Party's Over, Reconciliation, The Lighthouse), and a guest performance from Michael Sidwell (of Leithinis) on keyboards (The Lighthouse). Recorded, mixed, mastered and co-produced by David Jamison at Millstone Studios, County Down, Northern Ireland. The album was released on 30/1/2026.

The Lighthouse (Ghosts of the Mind)
This is an old family legend. One of my ancestors, a Protestant, fell deeply in love with a Catholic girl. Her family forbade the match, demanding he convert to Catholicism or end the relationship, but he refused to convert. Heartbroken, she let him go. Consumed by sorrow, he withdrew to the windswept Maiden Rocks off the coast of County Antrim, spending his days as a solitary lighthouse keeper, over the breaking sea.
